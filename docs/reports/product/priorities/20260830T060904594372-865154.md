## Priority: an honest "don't ship" is preferred over a rescue, on re-derived decisions (issue #2326)

Stated in this session's task brief, directing a redo of the ship/no-ship
call on PR #2855's lint-test-on-edit hook after PR #2859's independent
verification found the hook's test-selector misses the case it was built
for: "Redo the decision with those [corrected numbers]... If it cannot be
made to hit, say so and recommend not shipping the hook. This issue's Ask
is explicitly conditional and a lint-only hook is not what it asked for.
I would rather have that conclusion than a rescue."

Two standing implications for future re-derivation work (re-running a
diagnose-first decision after new evidence surfaces a problem with the
original build):

1. When a corrected/alternative technique is proposed to save a
   conditional deliverable, it must be built and measured to completion
   before counting as a fix — not reasoned about abstractly as
   "should work." This session built and timed two alternative
   test-selectors (path-prefix, import-graph) rather than arguing for
   either, and the concrete build is what surfaced the import-graph
   selector's budget-blowing fan-in on the exact module the traced
   episode edited.
2. A conditional Ask's scope should not be quietly narrowed to whatever
   trivially passes (here: a lint-only hook, once the test-impact step
   proved unworkable) — silently shipping the easy subset is a fail
   condition, not a compromise, when the issue explicitly asked for the
   test-impact behavior specifically.

Separately, the same task brief drew a line between fixing a build's
result (the ship decision) and fixing the shared measurement instrument
that produced the numbers behind it: `scripts/rework_fraction.py`'s
episode-boundary defect was to be fixed "regardless of which way you
land, because it corrupts the instrument rather than the hook... That
instrument will be reused; it should not carry that." Worth keeping as
a general split for future rework-measurement or similar-instrument
work: a build decision and the instrument that measured it are two
separable questions, and a defect in the instrument is not conditional
on the build's own outcome.
