- 2026-08-30: standing measurement-discipline principle surfaced while
  independently verifying issue #2837/PR #2839's pipeline-latency
  attribution: a zero (or exact) residual across independently-computed
  segments that are defined to telescope (`segment_a + segment_b =
  total` by construction, e.g. `S1a=(Es-E1)`, `S1b=(E2-Es)`) proves the
  arithmetic was executed correctly, not that the chosen boundary event
  (`Es` here) is the *right* boundary. Future measurement records in this
  repo that report a residual/closure check on a telescoping split should
  say explicitly which of the two the check demonstrates, rather than
  letting "closes exactly" read as validation of the segment definition
  itself. Source: docs/issue-2837/reports/adversarial-review-de1e46b2.md,
  "What was done" part 1.
- 2026-08-30: companion principle from the same verification: a
  keyword/heuristic attribution method (e.g. classifying tool calls by
  target-path/command substring into buckets like "record" vs "editing"
  vs "landing") can swing a headline ratio by 3x or more depending on
  category-priority choices, even when reading the identical transcript
  and reaching the same qualitative conclusion. Before presenting a
  single ratio from a heuristic classifier as *the* answer, future
  measurement records should either cross-check with an independently
  written classifier or explicitly flag the ratio as method-sensitive,
  not just data-sensitive. Source:
  docs/issue-2837/reports/adversarial-review-de1e46b2.md, "What was
  done" part 2 (#2811's ratio: 9.15x under one method, 3.06x under
  another, same transcript).
- 2026-08-30: companion principle from the same verification: before
  trusting a failure/retry count derived from a log's outcome field,
  check whether that log's outcome vocabulary can even represent the
  failure mode being counted. `runs/spawn-attempts.jsonl`'s
  `halted`/`session-log` vocabulary cannot represent "a session started,
  ran, and then self-refused" — it always records `session-log` for that
  case — so a count of `halted` outcomes systematically undercounts
  total disruptions whenever self-refusal is part of what "failure"
  means. Future counts drawn from this file should either cross-check
  against the underlying sessions' own `events.jsonl` `session-end`
  outcomes or state the undercount risk explicitly. Source:
  docs/issue-2837/reports/adversarial-review-de1e46b2.md, "What was
  done" part 3 (found 3 self-refused sessions in-window recorded as
  `session-log`, not 1, against the file's own single `halted` hit).
